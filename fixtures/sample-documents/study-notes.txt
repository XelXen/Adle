Study notes: gradient descent
(Sample notes written for Adle's tests.)

Gradient descent is a way of reducing a loss a little at a time. Start with any weights. Work out
which direction makes the loss go up fastest: that direction is the gradient. Then move the
weights a small step the opposite way.

The learning rate controls the size of the step. With a learning rate that is too high the loss
bounces around or even grows. With one that is too low the loss falls, but very slowly.

A useful picture is a ball rolling downhill. The slope under the ball is the gradient. The ball
always rolls towards lower ground, and it stops when the ground is flat.

Things to remember for the quiz:
1. The update rule is: new weight = old weight - learning rate x gradient.
2. A flat region has a gradient of zero, so the weights stop changing there.
3. Reaching a low point does not guarantee it is the lowest point overall.
