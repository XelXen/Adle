3.1 Overview

A rate of change says how fast one quantity changes when another one does. A car's speed is the rate at which its position changes with time, and the slope of a hill is the rate at which its height changes with distance. This chapter starts from the average rate over an interval and makes the interval shrink until what is left is the rate at a single point.

3.2 Average Rate of Change

Over an interval from a to b, the average rate of change of a function f is the change in its value divided by the change in its input: the difference f(b) minus f(a), divided by b minus a. On a graph it is the slope of the straight line through the two points, which is called a secant line.

A train that covers 120 kilometres in two hours has an average speed of 60 kilometres an hour, even if it stopped at a station on the way. The average hides what happened in between, and that is what the next idea recovers.

3.3 The Derivative as a Limit

Let the interval shrink. Keep a fixed and move b towards it: the secant line turns, and the slopes it takes settle on one number. That number is the derivative of f at a, the rate of change at the single point a, and the line it is the slope of is the tangent line.

When the slopes do not settle, for instance at a sharp corner of the graph, the function has no derivative there. A function with a derivative at every point of an interval is called differentiable on it.

3.4 Rules for Derivatives

Working out a limit every time would be slow, so a few rules do the work. The derivative of a constant is zero. The derivative of x raised to a power n is n times x raised to the power n minus one. The derivative of a sum is the sum of the derivatives, and a constant factor can be taken outside.

With these three rules the derivative of any polynomial can be written down at once, one term at a time.

3.5 Summary

The average rate of change over an interval is the slope of a secant line. The derivative at a point is the limit of those slopes as the interval shrinks to nothing, the slope of the tangent line, and a handful of rules gives the derivative of a polynomial without a limit.
